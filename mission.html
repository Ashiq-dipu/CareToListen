The 4 Somatic Pillars

We believe the human body is not merely an intellectual vehicle that carries our heads around. It is where our unprocessed grief, vigilance, and capacity for joy reside.

01
PHYSIOLOGY FIRST

Somatic Regulation Before Cognitive Narrative

When a human nervous system is in sympathetic fight-or-flight or dorsal-vagal freeze, the prefrontal cortex goes offline. You cannot "talk someone out" of a panic state.

That is why every recording session begins with physical stillness. We invite guests and listeners to drop their shoulders, notice their contact with the chair, and take two deep physiological sighs. We establish safety in the body before we dare ask the mind to remember.

Studio Practice: The 90-Second Sigh — Inhale deeply through the nose, take a second quick sharp inhale at the top, and release through mouth in a long, soft exhale.
02
NON-ANXIOUS PRESENCE

Witnessing Over Premature Problem-Solving

When someone tells us they are grieving or lonely, modern culture rushes to say: “Have you tried magnesium? Have you meditated? Everything happens for a reason.”

On Care To Listen, we call this what it really is: the listener’s anxiety disguised as helpfulness. We teach and practice the rare art of sitting beside someone in the dark without reaching for the light switch until they are ready.

Studio Practice: Replacing "At least you have..." with "I am holding this with you. Take all the time you need."
03
INTEGRATION

Clinical Rigor Stripped of Clinical Coldness

Academic psychology often sounds detached and clinical; pop wellness often sounds shallow and unscientific.

We stand directly at the intersection. Marcus Vance brings licensed clinical marital and family therapy experience, while Maya Lin brings the empathetic inquiry of veteran investigative journalism. We translate polyvagal theory, internal family systems (IFS), and neurobiology into heartfelt, relatable human truth.

Studio Practice: Every clinical theory is accompanied by a concrete relational translation for everyday life.
04
SAFETY PROTOCOL

Uncompromising Ethical & Crisis Safety

Audio is uniquely intimate: our voices are whispered directly into listeners' ears via headphones while walking alone or driving in traffic.

With that intimacy comes immense responsibility. We never end a difficult broadcast without offering grounded anchor points, grounding prompts, and immediate 24/7 crisis contacts. No listener is left stranded in dysregulation.

Studio Practice: 24/7 Crisis Hotline access (988 in USA/Canada) displayed prominently across all digital platforms.
TRY IT RIGHT NOW

A 90-Second Studio Regulation Practice

Pause whatever you are reading. Place both feet firmly flat on the floor. Allow your shoulders to drop away from your ears.

1

Double Inhale

Deep breath through nose, then top it off with a second sharp sip of air.

2

Extended Sigh

Open mouth gently and let out a long, unhurried audible sigh for 6 to 8 seconds.

3

Notice Ground

Feel the weight of gravity holding your chair. You do not have to carry the room.
